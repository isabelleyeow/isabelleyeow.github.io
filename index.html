Interactive Experience Research Project

Analysing Luni.com

Isabelle Yeow Jia Qi // s4160554

Analysis Questions:

1. What was the first thing you paid attention to when interacting with the experience?

The first thing I noticed was how interactive the whole scene felt, nearly every object shifted or reacted the moment my cursor hovered and passed over it. I realised that certain objects went further than a hover-reaction as clicking them triggered a short animated action, this drew me towards the website even more and I was interested what else I could discover.

2. Spend two minutes with the experience and create a list of each of your discrete actions.

  1. Moved the cursor from left to right to view the different perspectives of the landing page
  2. Hovered over every different section of the homepage to watch each "burst" of elements
  3. Clicked the pen and book at the back of the experience
  4. Hovered over the careers tab on the top right to see it change
  5. Clicked the first virtual world on the left
  6. Moved my cursor around and clicked on the balls
  7. Tried to shoot the basketball and football into the goals
  8. Scrolled down
  9. Pressed and clicked every object I could see
  10. Scrolled down further
  11. Clicked the punching bag multiple times
  12. Clicked everything else to see if it moved, like the egg in the pan
  13. Scrolled back to the main page and clicked the last section
  14. Swiped to see all the pictures before going back to home

3. What part of the experience did you spend the most time engaging with?

The Omada section because there was an interactive football and basketball that you could play with and score goals, I found it pretty entertaining and spent most of my time trying to shoot the balls into the goals.

4. What was the most common action in your two minute interaction with the experience?

Clicking on almost every object in view, hoping to trigger another hidden animation. Once I realised some objects were interactive and others weren't, clicking became a way of testing the whole scene rather than a means to an end which was what I really enjoyed about this website

5. What is your impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience?

My impression is that the goal was to introduce Luni, what they do and the range of what they offer such as their studio work, the omada games and career opportunities, through a playful 3D visual identity rather than a a text heavy explanation. The interactivity itself seems designed to show off Luni's creative and technical craft as much as to explain the company. The website speaks for what they have to offer themselves.

6. How does the interactive experience communicate this primary goal?

It uses distinct 3D scenes for each section so users are able to learn what Luni does by exploring a space rather than reading a description. The playful interactions directly demonstrate what Luni makes, for instance: being handed a football or basketball to play in the omada section shows users that they create games instead of just telling them. The consistent whimsical and low poly style across every section also communicates Luni's brand personality alongside factual content. A good balance and contrast between fun and formal.

7. What is your impression of how the experience should be interacted with over time? (For how long and how many different times)

My impression is that it's built for a single and fairly short but exploratory session, a few minutes of clicking, scrolling and hovering to discover each area rather than something you'd need to revisit repeatedly. It rewards curiosity within one sitting which is finding all the hidden animations rather than depth across many visits.

8. How does the interactive experience communicate how it should be interacted with over time?

The scroll prompt in the bottom left signals a guided and linear path through the page rather than an open ended sandbox. The hover triggered animations are short and self contained suggesting they're meant to be sampled briefly and moved past. The finite number of interactive props like the pen, ball, pan, gives the exploration a natural end point. Once you've found all of them, there's a sense of completion for that visit.

9. What other media forms (digital or otherwise) does the experience reference?

Within the site itself, it draws on video game conventions for example the physics-based football and basketball users can play around with, animated film visual style and pop up or storybook conventions.

10. What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should act when engaging with your research experience?

Every reference signals that users should act playfully and exploitatively rather than efficiently, the game like elements invites users to click and experiment freely as if they are in a game rather than scanning the page for information like a typical corporate site.

11. What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should feel when engaging with your research experience?

It's designed to make you feel curious, pulling on the same vibes as an animated film or a game so that users know Luni is associated with fun and imagination instead of feeling like a serious, formal tech company
